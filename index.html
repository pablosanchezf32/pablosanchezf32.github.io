Published Papers

Carbon Pricing and Inequality: A Normative Perspective

With Saki Bigio, Diego Känzig, and Conor Walsh

We study the sources and distribution of welfare losses from unexpected European carbon price changes. The results suggest that public opposition to carbon taxes may reflect legitimate distributional concerns, especially among younger, poorer, and less educated households.

Working Papers

The Political Economy of Public Debt and Electoral Polarization

With Diego Huerta

This paper develops a politico-economic theory linking public debt and political polarization. The model generates a stochastic debt-electoral cycle and speaks to the documented U-shaped relationship between public debt and right-wing radicalism across OECD countries.

Credit Access and Housing Quality

With Kwok Yan Chiu and Diego Cid

Using data from Mexico, this paper studies whether broader mortgage credit access can improve housing quality in developing countries. We combine empirical evidence with a heterogeneous-agent model featuring discrete housing choice and borrowing constraints.
